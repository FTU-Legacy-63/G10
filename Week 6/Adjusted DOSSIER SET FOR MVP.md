### **KHCN-01 — Trần Phương Anh, 35**

**A. Player-visible dossier**

> Trần Phương Anh is 35 years old, married, and lives in Bắc Ninh with her husband, a production engineer, their four-year-old son, and her mother-in-law, who looks after the boy during the week. The boy attends a private bilingual kindergarten, the family takes one trip abroad each year, and Phương Anh describes the household as "comfortable but careful". Spending on groceries, utilities, kindergarten fees and petrol runs to about 22 million VND a month.

> Phương Anh has worked for seven years at a Korean-owned electronics manufacturer in an industrial park in Bắc Ninh and is now its HR Manager, on a permanent contract. Her salary is 38 million VND a month; her bank statements show it is credited on the 5th of every month, and the company's salary confirmation letter agrees. The company also gives her an annual health check-up and a team-building trip, which she says she values more than a bonus.

> Two years ago she took out a bank loan to fit out and furnish the family's apartment and has repaid 10 million VND a month ever since, without a missed payment. She says she has no other debts and no credit cards. The credit bureau (CIC) places her in Group 1\.

> She now wants to buy a mid-size SUV priced at about 800 million VND, with 300 million in savings going towards it. She asks the bank for 500,000,000 VND over 60 months. She does not want to pledge the apartment or any other asset; the loan would rest on her salary and credit record alone.

> *Bank system note — estimated monthly installment for this request: 12,696,700 VND.*

**A2. Revealed via "Check CIC"**

> Group 1\. Active loans: (1) bank installment loan, 10,000,000 VND/month, on time. No other debts on record.

**B. Author-side key**

1. Unsecured → tenor cap 60 months, max age 70, 18%/year → r \= 1.5%/month  
2. Adjusted tenor \= min(60, 60, (70 − 35\) × 12 \= 420\) \= **60 months**  
3. PMT \= 500,000,000 × 0.015 ÷ \[1 − (1.015)⁻⁶⁰\] \= 7,500,000 ÷ 0.590704 ≈ **12,696,700**  
4. Income \= 38,000,000. Existing (CIC) \= 10,000,000  
5. **DTI** \= (10,000,000 \+ 12,696,700) ÷ 38,000,000 \= **59.7%** → ≤ 70% → **APPROVE**

**C. Correct decision:** Approve

**D. Decision-Consequence Card**

**Classification: Approve**

*Figures used*

* Income: 38,000,000 VND/month — her salary, her only recurring income; statements and employer letter agree.  
* Existing obligations (CIC): 10,000,000 VND/month — one bank loan, as declared.  
* Loan type: nothing is pledged, so this is an unsecured loan: 18% a year, maximum term 60 months. The requested 60 months fits, and her age (35) does not shorten it → installment 12,696,700 VND/month.  
* DTI: (10,000,000 \+ 12,696,700) ÷ 38,000,000 \= 59.7%, below the 70% line.

*Assessment:* With seven years of stable service and a fixed monthly salary, Phương Anh's income can be relied on in full. After adding her existing bank loan and the new car payment, total monthly debt service takes about 60% of income, leaving a real buffer for unplanned expenses. Reducing or rejecting this request would turn away a clearly affordable applicant. Her income and existing obligations alone are sufficient grounds for approval, independent of the car's price or her family circumstances.

### **KHCN-02 — Đỗ Văn Kiên, 41**

**A. Player-visible dossier**

> Đỗ Văn Kiên is 41 years old and lives in Ho Chi Minh City with his wife, their two children (aged 9 and 6\) and his widowed mother. His wife sews from home and earns about 7 million VND in a good month. The family rents an apartment and, all in, spends about 25 million a month on rent, groceries, school costs and his mother's medicine. On weekends Kiên coaches the youth football team at his children's school, and every summer the family spends two weeks in his wife's home village.

> For six years Kiên has been a Warehouse Supervisor at a logistics company in Ho Chi Minh City. His salary is 19.5 million VND a month and his bank statements show it arriving on the 10th of every month. He was named employee of the year in 2023\.

> He repays a bank loan of 5 million VND a month on a used family car, and a personal loan of 6 million a month that he took last year to pay for his mother's heart-valve surgery. He says both are up to date. CIC lists him in Group 1\. Over the years he has saved 400 million VND.

> The couple now wants to buy a townhouse in TP.HCM priced at 1.4 billion VND, because the landlord keeps raising the rent. Kiên would put in his 400 million in savings and asks the bank for 1,000,000,000 VND over 180 months. The bank would keep the townhouse's ownership certificate until the loan is fully repaid.

> *Bank system note — estimated monthly installment for this request: 11,366,100 VND.*

**A2. Revealed via "Check CIC"**

> Group 1\. Active loans: (1) bank auto loan, 5,000,000 VND/month, on time; (2) bank personal loan, 6,000,000 VND/month, on time. No other debts on record.

**B. Author-side key**

1. Secured → tenor cap 360 months, max age 70, 11%/year → r \= 0.9167%/month  
2. Adjusted tenor \= min(180, 360, (70 − 41\) × 12 \= 348\) \= **180 months**  
3. PMT \= 1,000,000,000 × 0.0091667 ÷ \[1 − (1.0091667)⁻¹⁸⁰\] \= 9,166,700 ÷ 0.806499 ≈ **11,366,100**  
4. Income \= 19,500,000. Existing (CIC) \= 11,000,000  
5. **DTI** \= (11,000,000 \+ 11,366,100) ÷ 19,500,000 \= **114.7%** → \> 80% → **REJECT**

**C. Correct decision:** Reject

**D. Decision-Consequence Card**

**Classification: Reject**

*Figures used*

* Income: 19,500,000 VND/month — his salary, his only recurring income.  
* Existing obligations (CIC): 5,000,000 \+ 6,000,000 \= 11,000,000 VND/month, as declared. This alone is 56% of his income.  
* Loan type: the bank would hold the townhouse's ownership certificate, so this is a secured loan: 11% a year, maximum term 360 months. The requested 180 months fits, and his age (41) would allow up to 348 → installment 11,366,100 VND/month.  
* DTI: (11,000,000 \+ 11,366,100) ÷ 19,500,000 \= 114.7%, above the 80% line.

*Assessment:* Despite a stable, long-tenured position with a fixed salary, Kiên's existing debt obligations alone already consume more than half of his monthly income. Adding the installment for this house purchase pushes total debt service to roughly 115% of what he earns in a month. This leaves no disposable income to cover the new obligation, let alone his other monthly needs. Approving this loan would require him to draw on funds entirely outside his income just to stay current. In this case, his existing financial commitments alone are sufficient grounds for rejection, independent of the property's value.

### **KHCN-03 — Trịnh Thị Hạnh, 44**

**A. Player-visible dossier**

> Trịnh Thị Hạnh is 44 and lives in Hải Phòng with her husband, who repairs motorbikes and earns about 14 million VND a month, their two children, who attend a private school costing 4 million a month in total, plus about 2.5 million a month for extra classes, and her widowed mother. The mother's medicine and care cost about 3 million a month, which Hạnh's younger brother, who works in Japan, sends straight to Hạnh’s account every month. The family spends roughly 18 million a month on daily living and usually travels during the Tết holiday, at a cost of about 30 million.

> For seven years Hạnh has run a furniture shop on a main road in the city, selling sofas, beds, wardrobes and dining sets that she buys ready-made from workshops in Bắc Ninh and Hà Nội. It is well known locally. She keeps her own books, and an accountant she hires files the shop's tax returns every quarter, which she says match her records. Last year's average monthly turnover was about 270 million VND, ranging from roughly 190 million in the slow summer months to about 380 million in the weeks before Tết. Buying stock costs her around 180 million a month. The shop rent is 24 million, the three staff cost 8 million each, and electricity, delivery and advertising take about 12 million. Last year she also sold a plot of inherited land for 900 million VND and used the money to finish the family house.

> She now wants to lease the unit next door and add a second product line of mattresses and bedding. She asks for 280,000,000 VND over 72 months on the strength of the shop's record alone, with no property pledged: about 60 million for the fit-out, 170 million for a first order of stock and 50 million for the lease deposit. She expects sales to grow by about a third once the expansion is finished.

> *Bank system note — estimated monthly installment for this request: 7,110,200 VND.*

**A2. Revealed via "Check CIC"**

> Group 2\. Active loans: (1) bank renovation loan, 10,000,000 VND/month, one late payment about five months ago, since regularized; (2) bank working-capital loan, 5,000,000 VND/month, opened eight months ago, on time.

**B. Author-side key**

1. Unsecured → tenor cap **60 months**, max age 70, 18%/year → r \= 1.5%/month  
2. Adjusted tenor \= min(requested **72**, product cap **60**, (70 − 44\) × 12 \= 312\) \= **60 months** (the installment in the system note already reflects this)  
3. PMT \= 280,000,000 × 0.015 ÷ \[1 − (1.015)⁻⁶⁰\] \= 4,200,000 ÷ 0.590704 ≈ **7,110,200**  
4. 270 − 180 − 24 − 24 − 12 \= 30,000,000. Existing (CIC) \= 15,000,000  
5. **DTI** \= (15,000,000 \+ 7,110,200) ÷ 30,000,000 \= **73.7%** → 70–80% → **REDUCE LIMIT**  
6. Max installment \= 70% × 30,000,000 − 15,000,000 \= **6,000,000**  
7. Max loan (PV, n \= 60, r \= 1.5%) \= 6,000,000 × 0.590704 ÷ 0.015 ≈ **236,282,000 VND** (grading tolerance ±5% on the installment: 5,700,000 – 6,300,000)

*Trap check (internal):* using turnover as income → DTI ≈ 8.2% (Approve); using the declared 10M instead of 15M → 57.0% (Approve).

**C. Correct decision:** Reduce limit (≈ 236,282,000 VND over the same 60 months)

**D. Decision-Consequence Card**

**Classification: Reduce limit**

*Figures used*

* Income: 270,000,000 turnover − 180,000,000 stock − 24,000,000 rent − 24,000,000 wages − 12,000,000 other costs \= 30,000,000 VND/month. Turnover itself is not income, and the seasonal highs and lows average out within the 270 million.   
* Existing obligations (CIC): 10,000,000 \+ 5,000,000 \= 15,000,000 VND/month. She declared only the first loan.  
* Loan type: no property is pledged, so this is an unsecured loan: 18% a year, maximum term 60 months. She asked for 72, so the term is capped at 60 → installment 7,110,200 VND/month.  
* DTI: (15,000,000 \+ 7,110,200) ÷ 30,000,000 \= 73.7%, between 70% and 80%.  
* Maximum installment at the 70% line: 0.70 × 30,000,000 − 15,000,000 \= 6,000,000 → maximum loan ≈ 236,282,000 VND over 60 months.

*Assessment:* Despite a well-established shop with 450 million VND in monthly turnover, the income that counts for lending is the 30 million left after costs. A second bank loan that she did not mention brings her existing obligations to 15 million, and the new installment takes total debt service to nearly 74% of income. This leaves little room to absorb a slow sales month. Approving the full 280 million would extend more credit than her income can comfortably service, and it would rest on a debt she never declared. The defensible offer is a smaller loan of about 236 million over the same 60-month term.

### **KHCN-04 — Nguyễn Văn Thịnh, 63**

**A. Player-visible dossier**

> Nguyễn Văn Thịnh is 63 and lives in Đà Nẵng with his wife, who runs a small tea stall and earns about 10 million VND in a good month. They help pay 3 million a month towards the school fees of a granddaughter, and every autumn they spend about 15 million on visiting their son in Hà Nội for two weeks. Thịnh calls himself "semi-retired", and spends most mornings practising calligraphy at a local club, which charges 500,000 a month. Household costs come to about 20 million a month, and the couple pay about 50 million a year for a private health-insurance plan.

> Nearly three years ago Thịnh retired after forty years as a teacher at a state secondary school in Đà Nẵng, and now receives a pension of 8 million VND a month, which Social Insurance transfers to his account on the same day each month; he holds the pension decision letter. He owns three ground-floor commercial units on a busy street near the river. Two are let to separate tenants on signed three-year leases, one paying 20 million a month and the other 17 million, both by bank transfer at the start of the month; the tenants left security deposits of 60 million and 51 million, which he must return when the leases end. The third has just been renovated and is being advertised at 22 million a month, but has no tenant yet. Last year he also sold family farmland in his home village for 1.2 billion VND.

> The renovation of the third unit was paid for with a bank loan on which he pays 11 million a month; he tells the bank it is his only debt. The credit bureau lists him in Group 1\.

> Thịnh now wants to buy a small apartment in the city centre priced at 1.3 billion VND to rent out, which he expects to bring in around 15 million a month. He will put in 350 million from his land sale and offer the ownership certificate of one of his let units to the bank as security for 950,000,000 VND over 120 months.

> *Bank system note — estimated monthly installment for this request: 16,266,600 VND.*

**A2. Revealed via "Check CIC"**

> Group 1\. Active loans: (1) bank renovation loan, 11,000,000 VND/month, on time; (2) bank personal loan, 7,000,000 VND/month, opened 14 months ago, on time.

**B. Author-side key**

1. Income \= 8,000,000 \+ 20,000,000 \+ 17,000,000 \= 45,000,000.   
2. Secured → tenor cap 360 months, max age 70, 11%/year → r \= 0.9167%/month  
3. Adjusted tenor \= min(requested **120**, product cap 360, (70 − 63\) × 12 \= **84**) \= **84 months** (the age-to-70 cap is the binding limit)  
4. PMT \= 950,000,000 × 0.0091667 ÷ \[1 − (1.0091667)⁻⁸⁴\] \= 8,708,365 ÷ 0.535352 ≈ **16,266,600**  
5. Income \= 45,000,000. Existing (CIC) \= 18,000,000  
6. **DTI** \= (18,000,000 \+ 16,266,600) ÷ 45,000,000 \= **76.1%** → 70–80% → **REDUCE LIMIT**  
7. Max installment \= 70% × 45,000,000 − 18,000,000 \= **13,500,000**  
8. Max loan (PV, n \= 84, r \= 0.9167%) \= 13,500,000 × 0.535352 ÷ 0.0091667 ≈ **788,427,000 VND** (grading tolerance ±5% on the installment: 12,825,000 – 14,175,000)

*Trap check (internal):* using the declared 11M instead of 18M → 60.6% (Approve); ignoring the age cap and using 120 months → installment ≈ 13,086,300, DTI ≈ 69.1% (Approve); counting the expected rent and his wife's tea-stall income → ≈ 51.9% (Approve); counting the advertised 22M rent of the vacant unit → (18M \+ 16.27M) ÷ (45M \+ 22M) ≈ 51.1% (Approve).

**C. Correct decision:** Reduce limit (≈ 788,427,000 VND over 84 months)

**D. Decision-Consequence Card**

**Classification: Reduce limit**

*Figures used*

* Income: pension 8,000,000 \+ rents 20,000,000 \+ 17,000,000 \= 45,000,000 VND/month.  
* Existing obligations (CIC): 11,000,000 \+ 7,000,000 \= 18,000,000 VND/month. He declared only the first loan.  
* Loan type: he offers a property certificate as security, so this is a secured loan: 11% a year, maximum term 360 months. He asked for 120 months, but at 63 the loan must mature by age 70: (70 − 63\) × 12 \= 84 months → installment 16,266,600 VND/month.  
* DTI: (18,000,000 \+ 16,266,600) ÷ 45,000,000 \= 76.1%, between 70% and 80%.  
* Maximum installment at the 70% line: 0.70 × 45,000,000 − 18,000,000 \= 13,500,000 → maximum loan ≈ 788,427,000 VND over 84 months.

*Assessment:* Despite a stable pension and two long-term tenants, Thịnh's age limits this loan to 84 months rather than the 120 he asked for, which raises the monthly installment. With a second bank loan he did not mention, total debt service reaches 76% of his income. Neither the rent he expects from the new apartment nor the advertised rent of the vacant unit can be counted before he receives it, and the tenants' deposits are not his income. Approving the full 950 million at the requested term would rely on a term he is not eligible for and on income he does not yet have. The defensible offer is a smaller loan of about 788 million over 84 months.

### **KHCN-05 — Vũ Minh Tuấn, 39 — Hard**

**A. Player-visible dossier**

> Vũ Minh Tuấn is 39, divorced, and lives alone in a rented one-bedroom apartment in Ho Chi Minh City. His six-year-old daughter lives with her mother; Tuấn pays her school fees of about 6 million VND a month, plus about 1.5 million for after-school classes. His own living costs come to about 20 million a month, of which the rent is 12 million (he paid a three-month deposit of 36 million when he moved in). He plays golf once a month at about 2.5 million, and takes a beach holiday every summer that costs about 15 million.

> For five years Tuấn has been a sales consultant at a real-estate distribution company in Ho Chi Minh City, selling apartments in new projects. His contract sets a fixed base of 9 million VND a month, and he earns a commission when each sale closes. His payslips for the last six months show commissions from the closed deals that paid him 53 million, 35 million, 21 million and 48 million VND on top of the base; in the other two months nothing was paid. He tells the bank he earns “around 50 million a month”. He has three more deals in the pipeline that he says should pay around 120 million in total. Last month he also sold his old car for 470 million VND; he had bought it three years earlier for 640 million.

> Two years ago he took a bank personal loan to clear credit-card debts. He pays 10 million VND a month on it and says it is his only debt. The credit bureau lists him in Group 2 because of one late payment about four months ago.

> He now wants to buy a new car priced at 830 million VND, because he says clients judge a property consultant by the car he drives. On top of the price come about 95 million in registration fees and 12 million in first-year insurance, which he says he will pay himself. He will put in the 470 million from selling his old car and asks the bank for 360,000,000 VND over 60 months, without pledging any asset.

> *Bank system note — estimated monthly installment for this request: 9,141,600 VND.*

**A2. Revealed via "Check CIC"**

> Group 2\. Active loans: (1) bank personal loan, 10,000,000 VND/month, one late payment about four months ago, since regularized; (2) second bank personal loan (different bank), 11,000,000 VND/month, opened 10 months ago, on time.

**B. Author-side key**

1. Unsecured → tenor cap 60 months, max age 70, 18%/year → r \= 1.5%/month  
2. Adjusted tenor \= min(60, 60, (70 − 39\) × 12 \= 372\) \= **60 months**  
3. PMT \= 360,000,000 × 0.015 ÷ \[1 − (1.015)⁻⁶⁰\] \= 5,400,000 ÷ 0.590704 ≈ **9,141,600**  
4. Income \= 9,000,000 \+ 27,000,000 \= 36,000,000. Existing (CIC) \= 21,000,000  
5. **DTI** \= (21,000,000 \+ 9,141,600) ÷ 36,000,000 \= **83.7%** → \> 80% → **REJECT**

*Trap check (internal):* averaging only the four paid months (commission 40.5M, income 49.5M) → 60.9% (Approve); trusting his stated 50M → 60.3% (Approve); correct income but the declared 10M → 53.2% (Approve); using his best month (9 \+ 58 \= 67M) → 45.0% (Approve). Only the full reading gives Reject.

**C. Correct decision:** Reject

**D. Decision-Consequence Card**

**Classification: Reject**

*Figures used*

* Income: fixed base 9,000,000 \+ commissions (58 \+ 0 \+ 35 \+ 21 \+ 0 \+ 48 \= 162,000,000 over six months \= 27,000,000 a month) \= 36,000,000 VND/month. The two empty months are part of what he actually receives, and his stated 50 million is not supported by his payslips.  
* Existing obligations (CIC): 10,000,000 \+ 11,000,000 \= 21,000,000 VND/month. He declared only the first loan.  
* Loan type: nothing is pledged, so this is an unsecured loan: 18% a year, maximum term 60 months → installment 9,141,600 VND/month.  
* DTI: (21,000,000 \+ 9,141,600) ÷ 36,000,000 \= 83.7%, above the 80% line.

Assessment: Despite a fixed base and a good month that paid 58 million VND in commission, Tuấn's income is the 36 million he averages once the two empty months are included. A second bank loan he did not mention brings his existing obligations to 21 million, and the new installment takes total debt service to about 84% of his average income. Averaging only his four paid months, or relying on deals that have not closed, would have made this look affordable; neither reflects what he actually receives each month. Approving would leave him dependent on the next sale just to stay current. His actual financial position alone is sufficient grounds for rejection.
