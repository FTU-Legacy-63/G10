**ASSUMPTIONS (Owner: Lai Ngoc Linh)**

* **DTI-based decision rule with one set of bands –** DTI \= (Existing obligations \+ New installment) ÷ Income (verified), classified into three fixed bands: ≤70% Approve, 70–80% Reduce limit, \>80% Reject. The same lines apply to every dossier; risk signals never move them.  
- *Disclosure:* the player is not given the DTI; the player calculates it from the dossier, and the system’s DTI appears only on the Summary Consequence Card at the end of the round.  
* **Stated vs. Verified income –** Every dossier may show Stated income; Verified income lives in a separate Warehouse lookup. The two figures are sometimes independent and may differ. The player should check the Warehouse for every case, not only ones that look suspicious.  
- *Risk:* if too few authored cases actually have a mismatch, players may learn to skip the Warehouse check entirely, defeating the mechanic’s purpose.  
* **Character is CIC groups, inquiries and a document check –** Block (Reject) if the current CIC group is 2 or worse, or the document check shows forgery signs. Watch if the current group is 1 but the worst group in the last 12 months is 2 or worse, or 3 or more lenders inquired in the last month. Clear otherwise. A gap between Stated and Verified income is not forgery; it only changes the DTI.  
- *Risk:* the 12-month window and the 3-inquiries-in-a-month level come from the thesis. Reading “overdue debt” as a current group of 2 or worse is our assumption. The document check is a field the dossier author sets, and there is no event-level payment history and no CIC of a spouse or guarantor.  
* **Warning signals lead to Add conditions, not to a change of the DTI lines –** A Character Watch, a Capital Watch or an Adverse environment makes an otherwise approvable file an Add conditions decision: the reason is recorded and conditions are attached.  
- *Risk:* in the thesis the appraiser records the reason and the approving level decides. Mapping that to Add conditions is a design choice, because a game answer must be deterministic. A real approver could also reject or reduce the limit.  
* **Capital is liquid reserves only –** Reserve cover \= Liquid assets ÷ (Existing obligations \+ New installment). Under 3 months is a Watch signal; otherwise Clear. A high reserve never loosens any rule. Assets pledged to the loan are excluded from Liquid assets so the same asset is not counted as both Capital and Collateral.  
- *Risk:* the thesis has no rule for an individual’s own capital, so the 3-month level is a design assumption to be confirmed with the finance expert. There is no net-worth calculation.  
* **Collateral is accepted first, then tested on LTV (Secured loans only) –** The pledged real estate must be legally owned with no dispute and must be sellable; otherwise the file is rejected. If accepted, Net collateral value \= Appraised value − Senior claims outstanding, and LTV \= Loan amount ÷ Net collateral value is compared with a 70% Max LTV. The appraised value is pre-set in the dossier; the player does not appraise.  
- *Risk:* the acceptance requirements are sourced from the thesis. The 70% Max LTV and the deduction of senior claims are assumptions, since the thesis gives no LTV and each bank sets its own. Only real estate and one asset per loan are modelled.  
* **A collateral shortfall has one answer –** If LTV is above the Max LTV, the decision is Require more collateral, and the borrower cures it only by pledging additional collateral. Reduce limit stays a decision for DTI alone; it is never the answer to a collateral shortfall, and the Summary Card does not show a reduced loan amount as an alternative.  
- *Risk:* the thesis is silent on how a shortfall is cured. A real bank may also offer a smaller loan; the game removes that option so each dossier keeps one designed answer and Reduce limit is not confused with the collateral rule.  
* **Conditions are a purpose check plus the operating environment –** If the documents do not support the purpose or the amount, the file is rejected. Otherwise the environment is Adverse when the industry outlook is Unfavorable or the macro flag is Stressed, which adds conditions; the DTI lines do not change. The macro flag is set once per round by the game designer and is the same for every dossier.  
- *Risk:* the purpose rule is sourced (an unfeasible purpose is a listed rejection reason); the Adverse rule is an assumption. The macro flag does not change within a round.  
* **Conditions come from post-disbursement checks –** The menu is: documents showing the loan was used for its purpose; periodic re-appraisal of the collateral (Secured only); periodic update of the borrower’s occupation and income. Conditions never change the loan amount or the DTI.  
- *Risk:* the checks are sourced from the thesis; turning them into a menu of conditions is a design choice. Which condition fits which case is not prescribed, so the player’s choice is feedback only.  
* **One headline decision, by fixed order –** Reject, then Reduce limit, then Require more collateral, then Add conditions, then Approve. If two triggers apply, the higher one is the answer and the other appears on the Summary Card as a secondary requirement. Each dossier is designed so that only one non-Approve trigger binds.  
- *Risk:* the order (most restrictive outcome first) is a design choice.  
* **Deterministic consequence –** Each dossier still has one designed, correct outcome. The decision inputs only add a player-entered number before that outcome is revealed – it is still not a probabilistic simulation.  
* **Loan Type / Product Parameters –** Loan Type is Unsecured or Secured only; it routes to a fixed table of tenor cap, maximum age, and interest rate (Unsecured: 60 months / age 70 / 18%; Secured: 360 months / age 70 / 11%), and decides whether Collateral is tested. It does not change the DTI decision bands.  
- *Risk:* the interest rates are assumptions; the lower secured rate stands in for the lower loss severity of secured lending.  
* **Age capped through tenor, not through the approved amount –** Adjusted tenor \= min(requested tenor, product’s max tenor, (max age at maturity − current age) × 12). A shorter tenor raises the installment – and therefore the DTI; age never scales the approved amount directly.  
- *Risk:* the same maximum age (70) is applied to both Unsecured and Secured products in this MVP; real banks may vary this by product.  
* **Installment formula –** New loan monthly installment uses the standard amortization formula P × r ÷ \[1−(1+r)^−n\], not requested amount ÷ tenor. The Reduce-limit input inverts this same formula (Present Value) to convert the player’s entered installment back into a loan amount.  
- *Risk:* none regulatory – chosen because the old straight-line approximation understated real debt service. The thesis also counts the monthly payment on granted card and overdraft limits as debt service; this MVP counts only the obligations stated in the dossier.  
* **Decision inputs are hand calculations with a tolerance –** Reduce limit: the player enters Max installment \= 70% × Income − Existing obligations. Require more collateral: the player enters Loan amount ÷ Max LTV − Net collateral value, and the system rounds its own figure up to the nearest 1,000,000 VND. Add conditions: the player selects at least one condition. Numeric inputs are graded within ±5%.  
- *Risk:* the ±5% tolerance and the rounding step are UX assumptions.  
* **Review first, feedback at the end –** Decisions are provisional until the player submits the round. The player can reopen any dossier already opened and revise the worksheet, decision or decision input; no correctness is shown until the single Summary Consequence Card after submission, and only the final decision on each dossier is assessed. Room used is recalculated from the current decisions.  
- *Risk:* because nothing is revealed before submission, revising cannot be used to peek at answers. The trade-offs are that the player learns nothing from an individual case until the end, and that revising earlier decisions after seeing later dossiers changes how room is allocated. Review covers opened dossiers only, so there is no preview of upcoming ones.  
* **Worksheet is feedback only –** The worksheet (Character rating, purpose result, acceptance result, Net collateral value, LTV, Reserve cover, Capital rating, Conditions rating, DTI) may be left blank and does not change the decision score; blank fields show as “not answered” on the Summary Card.  
- *Risk:* players may skip it; how it feeds the scorecard is left to the scorecard design.  
* **Simulated dossiers –** unchanged. All applicants are fictional, based on reality.  
  –     *Reason:* privacy and feasibility.
